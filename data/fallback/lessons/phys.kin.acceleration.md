## The idea
Acceleration tells us how quickly velocity changes. It is defined as  

\[
a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t},
\]

the change in velocity divided by the time over which that change occurs.  
A key point is that acceleration does **not** require the object to be moving. Even if the velocity is zero at an instant, the object can still be accelerating if its speed is about to increase or decrease.

---

## Why your answer happened
You wrote that the acceleration must be zero because the ball’s velocity was zero at the highest point of its trajectory. That reasoning feels natural—if something isn’t moving, it seems like nothing could be “speeding up” or “slowing down.” The slip comes from mixing up **instantaneous velocity** (which can be zero) with **instantaneous acceleration** (which depends on how the velocity is changing, not on its current value).

---

## Analogy
Think of a sprinter at the start line.  
- The sprinter’s **position** is like the ball’s location.  
- The sprinter’s **speed** is the ball’s velocity.  
- The sprinter’s **push off the blocks** is the acceleration.

At the exact moment the sprinter’s feet are still on the blocks, his speed is zero, but the force he’s applying (the push) is huge—that’s the acceleration. Likewise, a ball at the top of its arc has zero speed, yet gravity is still pulling it down, giving it a constant downward acceleration of $9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.

---

## Worked example
A ball is thrown straight up with an initial speed of $20\ \text{m/s}$.  
Take upward as positive. Gravity provides a constant acceleration $a=-9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.

1. **Find the time to reach the highest point**  
   At the top, $v=0$. Use  

   \[
   v = v_0 + at \quad\Rightarrow\quad 0 = 20 + (-9.8)t
   \]

   \[
   t = \frac{20}{9.8}\approx 2.04\ \text{s}.
   \]

2. **What is the acceleration at that instant?**  
   Acceleration does not depend on the instantaneous velocity, so  

   \[
   a = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2
   \]

   even though $v=0$ at $t\approx2.04\ \text{s}$.

Thus the ball is still accelerating downward while momentarily at rest.

## Try this
A soccer player kicks a ball straight up, and at the very top of its flight the ball’s speed is $0\ \text{m/s}$. What is the ball’s acceleration at that instant?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
The acceleration is $-9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ (downward), because gravity acts continuously regardless of the ball’s instantaneous speed.
