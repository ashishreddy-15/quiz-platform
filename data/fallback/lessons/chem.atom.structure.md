## The idea
An atom consists of a tiny, dense nucleus made of protons (positive) and neutrons (neutral). Electrons orbit this nucleus in defined regions called shells. The nucleus holds most of the atom’s mass, while electrons determine chemical behavior.

## Why your answer happened
Many students answer that electrons sit inside the nucleus because they picture the atom as a ball with a solid core. This feels reasonable because in sports, a ball’s core is solid and the outer layers are visible, so it’s easy to think all parts are inside the core. In reality, the nucleus is separate from the electron “cloud,” which surrounds it.

## Analogy
Think of a soccer team:  
- **The nucleus** is like the **team captain**—central, powerful, and holds the team together.  
- **Electrons** are like the **players** running around the field, always moving but staying outside the captain’s immediate space.  
- **Protons** are the **captain’s teammates** who support him, while **neutrons** are the **coach’s assistants** who keep the team stable.  
- The **electron shells** are the **positions** players occupy on the field; each position has a specific role.

## Worked example
We want the electron configuration of oxygen (Z = 8).  
1. Fill the first shell (K) with 2 electrons:  
   $$1s^2$$  
2. Fill the second shell (L) with the remaining 6 electrons, filling the 2s and then 2p orbitals:  
   $$2s^2 2p^4$$  
So, oxygen’s configuration is $$1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4$$.
