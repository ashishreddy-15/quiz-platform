## The idea
A **force** is a push or pull that acts on an object. It has both a magnitude (how strong) and a direction (which way). In physics, we write forces as vectors, like \( \vec{F} = 5\,\text{N}\,\hat{i} \). The size tells us how hard the force is, and the arrow shows the direction.

## Why your answer happened
Many students think a force is only the *magnitude* because they see numbers like “10 N” and ignore the arrow. In a sports example, you might say “the ball gets 10 N of force,” but you didn’t say which way the ball is pushed. That omission makes the answer feel incomplete, yet it looks reasonable if you focus only on the number.

## Analogy
Think of a soccer ball in a game.  
- **The ball** is the object being acted on.  
- **The kicker’s foot** applies a force.  
- **The number of kicks (10 N)** is the magnitude.  
- **The direction of the kick** (forward, left, right) is the direction of the vector.  
Just as a kick can send a ball forward or sideways, a force can push or pull in any direction.

## Worked example
A player pushes a 2 kg sled with a force of \(12\,\text{N}\) to the right.  
1. Write the force vector:  
   \[
   \vec{F} = 12\,\text{N}\,\hat{i}
   \]
2. Use Newton’s second law \( \vec{F} = m\vec{a} \).  
3. Solve for acceleration:  
   \[
   \vec{a} = \frac{\vec{F}}{m} = \frac{12\,\text{N}\,\hat{i}}{2\,\text{kg}} = 6\,\text{m/s}^2\,\hat{i}
   \]
The sled accelerates to the right at \(6\,\text{m/s}^2\).

## Try this
A basketball player shoots the ball upward with a force of \(30\,\text{N}\).  
What is the direction of the force?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
The force points upward, opposite the direction of gravity.
