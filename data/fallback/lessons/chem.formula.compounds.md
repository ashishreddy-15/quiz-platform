## The idea  
In a chemical formula the number written after an element symbol (the subscript) tells how many atoms of that element are present in one molecule of the compound. If no number appears, it means one atom. For example, in \( \mathrm{H_2O} \) the subscript 2 after H indicates two hydrogen atoms, and the absence of a number after O means one oxygen atom.

## Why your answer happened  
You probably omitted a subscript or wrote the wrong number, which is common when you think the symbol itself represents the quantity. For instance, writing \( \mathrm{H_2O} \) as \( \mathrm{H_2O_2} \) or \( \mathrm{HO} \) feels reasonable if you think “H” and “O” alone already show the amounts. Remember that the subscript is the key indicator of how many atoms of each element are in the molecule.

## Analogy  
Think of a sports team lineup: the team name is like the chemical formula, the player names are the element symbols, and the number next to each player’s name is the subscript telling you how many of that player are on the field. If you list “Goalkeeper” without a number, it means exactly one goalkeeper. If you write “Striker 2”, that means two strikers on the field.

## Worked example  
Determine the formula for a compound that contains 4 carbon atoms, 8 hydrogen atoms, and 2 oxygen atoms.

1. Write each element symbol:  
   \( \mathrm{C}\), \( \mathrm{H}\), \( \mathrm{O} \).

2. Attach the appropriate subscript for each:  
   \( \mathrm{C_4}\), \( \mathrm{H_8}\), \( \mathrm{O_2} \).

3. Combine them in any order:  
   $$\mathrm{C_4H_8O_2}$$

This is the chemical formula for the compound.
