## The idea  
A unit rate tells you how many of one thing you get for exactly one of another thing.  
For example, if a soccer team scores 6 goals in 3 matches, the unit rate is  
\[
\frac{6\text{ goals}}{3\text{ matches}} = 2\text{ goals per match}.
\]
The “per match” part means “for each single match.”  

## Why your answer happened  
Many students write the ratio 6 : 3 and think that’s the answer. That shows the confusion between a *ratio* and a *unit rate*. A ratio compares two quantities, while a unit rate gives the comparison for a single unit of the denominator.  

## Analogy  
Think of a basketball player’s free‑throw percentage.  
*The “free throws made” is like the numerator (what you have).  
*The “free‑throw attempts” is like the denominator (what you compare to).  
*The “per attempt” part is the unit rate, telling you how many shots you make for each single attempt.  

## Worked example  
Suppose a baseball pitcher throws 90 pitches in 5 innings.  
1. Write the ratio:  
   \[
   \frac{90\text{ pitches}}{5\text{ innings}}
   \]
2. Divide both numbers by the denominator (5) to get one inning:  
   \[
   \frac{90\div5}{5\div5} = \frac{18}{1}
   \]
3. Express as a unit rate:  
   \[
   18\text{ pitches per inning}
   \]
   So the pitcher throws 18 pitches for every single inning.  

## Try this  
How many goals does a soccer team score on average if they score 14 goals in 7 matches?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
\[
\frac{14}{7}=2\text{ goals per match}
\]</details>
