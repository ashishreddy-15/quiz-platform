## The idea  
Speed measures how fast an object moves, ignoring direction; it is a scalar.  
Velocity tells how fast and in which direction the object moves; it is a vector.  
Mathematically, velocity is the derivative of position with respect to time:  
\[
\vec v(t)=\frac{d\vec r(t)}{dt}.
\]  

## Why your answer happened  
Many students answer “speed equals velocity” because both words describe motion.  
In the missing question you likely treated speed as if it had direction, which is a common error.  
It feels reasonable because we often talk about “running fast” and think direction is part of that description, but mathematically speed lacks a direction component.

## Analogy  
Think of a soccer player sprinting toward the goal.  
- The **speed** is how many meters per second the player covers, like the number of kicks per minute.  
- The **velocity** is that same rate **plus** the direction toward the goal.  
So, “the kicks per minute” (speed) is like the number of balls you throw, while “the kicks per minute toward the goal” (velocity) is like throwing those balls straight into the net.

## Worked example  
A car travels 120 km north in 2 h, then 80 km south in 1 h.  
1. **Speed**:  
   \[
   \text{Total distance}=120+80=200\text{ km}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Total time}=2+1=3\text{ h}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Average speed}=\frac{200}{3}\approx66.7\text{ km/h}
   \]
2. **Velocity**:  
   Net displacement = 120 km north – 80 km south = 40 km north.  
   \[
   \text{Average velocity}=\frac{40\text{ km north}}{3\text{ h}}
   \approx13.3\text{ km/h north}
   \]
So the car’s speed is 66.7 km/h, but its velocity is 13.3 km/h toward the north.

## Try this  
A runner covers 400 m in 60 s, then turns and runs 200 m back toward the start in 30 s.  
What is the runner’s average speed and average velocity?  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>
Average speed = \(\frac{400+200}{60+30}= \frac{600}{90}=6.67\) m/s.  
Net displacement = 200 m forward – 200 m back = 0 m, so average velocity = \(0\) m/s.  
</details>
