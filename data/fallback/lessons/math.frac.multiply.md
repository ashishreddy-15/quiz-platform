## The idea  
When you multiply two fractions, you multiply the top numbers (numerators) together and the bottom numbers (denominators) together.  
\[
\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}= \frac{a\cdot c}{\,b\cdot d\,}
\]

## Why your answer happened  
Many students think “multiply the whole fractions as if they were whole numbers.” For example, they might write  
\[
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{4}= \frac{2\times3}{3\times4}= \frac{6}{12}
\]
and then simplify incorrectly to \(\frac{1}{2}\).  
This feels reasonable because they see the fractions side‑by‑side and think “just multiply everything.” The mistake is not recognizing that the numerators and denominators must be paired separately.

## Analogy  
Think of a sports team where each player has a jersey number (numerator) and a position number (denominator).  
- The **jersey numbers** are like the numerators.  
- The **position numbers** are like the denominators.  
When you combine two teams, you match every player’s jersey number with every other player’s jersey number, and the same for positions. You never mix a jersey number with a position number.  

## Worked example  
Multiply \(\displaystyle \frac{5}{6}\) by \(\displaystyle \frac{2}{9}\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{2}{9}
&= \frac{5\cdot 2}{6\cdot 9} \\
&= \frac{10}{54} \\
&= \frac{5}{27}\quad\text{(after dividing numerator and denominator by }2).
\end{aligned}
\]

Notice we never mixed 5 with 9 or 6 with 2.

## Try this  
What is \(\displaystyle \frac{4}{7}\times\frac{3}{5}\)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
\[
\frac{4}{7}\times\frac{3}{5}= \frac{4\cdot3}{7\cdot5}= \frac{12}{35}
\]
</details>
