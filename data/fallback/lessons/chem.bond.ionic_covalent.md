## The idea  
In an **ionic bond** one atom donates one or more electrons to another atom, creating oppositely charged ions that attract.  
In a **covalent bond** two atoms share electrons, keeping the pair between them so both atoms achieve a stable configuration.

## Why your answer happened  
Many students think “ionic bonds are just a type of covalent bond” because both involve electrons. That idea is tempting: after all, both bonds involve moving or sharing electrons. However, the key difference is that ionic bonds involve a *transfer* of electrons, producing charged ions, while covalent bonds involve a *sharing* of electrons, keeping the atoms neutral.

## Analogy  
Think of a soccer match.  
- **Ionic bond**: One player (atom) kicks the ball (electron) to a teammate (another atom). The ball changes possession, and the players now have opposite roles—one is “donor” (positive) and the other “acceptor” (negative).  
- **Covalent bond**: Two teammates hold the ball together, passing it back and forth. They both keep the ball in play, sharing its control without giving it up entirely.

## Worked example  
Consider sodium (Na) and chlorine (Cl).  
1. Sodium has one valence electron: $\ce{Na -> Na+ + e^-}$  
2. Chlorine needs one electron: $\ce{Cl + e^- -> Cl-}$  
3. The ions attract: $\ce{Na+ + Cl- -> NaCl}$  

In contrast, for hydrogen gas:  
1. Each H atom has one electron.  
2. They share the pair: $\ce{H + H -> H2}$ (no charge transfer).
