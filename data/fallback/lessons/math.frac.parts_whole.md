## The idea
A fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ tells us how many equal pieces we have taken from a whole that has been split into $b$ pieces. The top number $a$ (the numerator) counts the pieces we keep, and the bottom number $b$ (the denominator) tells how many pieces the whole was divided into. If $a<b$, the fraction is a part of the whole; if $a=b$, it is the whole; if $a>b$, we have more than one whole.

## Why your answer happened
You wrote the fraction as the “size of each piece” instead of the “number of pieces taken.” That feels natural because when we look at a picture of a pizza cut into slices, each slice looks smaller when there are more slices. It’s easy to mix up “how big each slice is” with “how many slices we have.”

## Analogy
Think about a basketball game.  
- The **whole game** is like the entire basketball court.  
- **Dividing the court into quarters** is like splitting the game into 4 equal time periods (the denominator $b$).  
- **Scoring in one quarter** is like taking $a$ quarters of the game (the numerator $a$).  

So $\dfrac{2}{4}$ means you played (or scored) in 2 of the 4 quarters, which is the same as half the game.

## Worked example
Suppose a chocolate bar is broken into 8 equal pieces. You eat 3 pieces.

1. Identify the denominator: the bar was split into $8$ pieces → $b=8$.
2. Identify the numerator: you ate $3$ pieces → $a=3$.
3. Write the fraction: $\displaystyle \frac{3}{8}$.

If you wanted to know what part of the bar is left, subtract the eaten pieces:
$$\text{Remaining pieces}=8-3=5,$$
so the remaining fraction is
$$\frac{5}{8}.$$

## Try this
