## The idea  
Position tells you where an object is at a specific instant, measured from a chosen reference point.  
Displacement is the vector that connects the initial position to the final position; it has both magnitude and direction.  

## Why your answer happened  
Many students think “position” and “displacement” mean the same because both involve location. In the question you likely answered “the ball’s location after it was kicked” for displacement, which sounds reasonable but ignores that displacement is the change from start to finish, not the final spot itself.  

## Analogy  
Imagine a soccer player starts at the penalty spot.  
- *Position* is like the player’s exact spot on the field at any moment (e.g., 30 m from the goal).  
- *Displacement* is like the straight‑line path the ball would travel if it moved directly from the penalty spot to where it ends up (e.g., 25 m toward the goal). The player’s final position is the endpoint; displacement is the vector that connects start to end.  

## Worked example  
A ball starts at \(x_0 = 0\) m and ends at \(x_f = 30\) m on a straight line.  
Displacement:  
\[
\Delta x = x_f - x_0 = 30\,\text{m} - 0\,\text{m} = 30\,\text{m}
\]  
Position at the end:  
\[
x_f = 30\,\text{m}
\]  
If the ball then moves back to \(x = 10\) m, the new displacement from the original start is  
\[
\Delta x = 10\,\text{m} - 0\,\text{m} = 10\,\text{m}
\]  
but the displacement from the previous position (30 m) is  
\[
\Delta x = 10\,\text{m} - 30\,\text{m} = -20\,\text{m}
\]  
(the negative sign shows direction toward the start).  

## Try this  
What is the displacement of a runner who starts at the 0 m mark, runs to the 100 m line, and then stops at the 70 m mark?  
<details><summary>Answer</summary>  
Displacement from start to finish: \(70\,\text{m} - 0\,\text{m} = 70\,\text{m}\).  
If you consider the displacement from the 100 m mark to the stop, it is \(-30\,\text{m}\).  
</details>
