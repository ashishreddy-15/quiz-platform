## The idea  
Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance. It equals the sum of the atomic masses of every atom in the formula, each multiplied by its subscript. For a compound \( \text{C}_x\text{H}_y\text{O}_z \) the molar mass is  
\[
M = x\,M_{\text{C}} + y\,M_{\text{H}} + z\,M_{\text{O}} .
\]

## Why your answer happened  
Many students treat the subscript as a multiplier for the *whole* formula instead of for each element. For example, writing \(2(\text{C}_3\text{H}_8)\) instead of \(6\text{C} + 16\text{H}\) gives a wrong value. This feels reasonable because the subscript is written next to the whole formula, but mathematically it applies to the individual element symbols.

## Analogy  
Think of a soccer team. The team’s total score is the sum of each player’s points. The number of players (subscript) tells you how many times that player’s score counts. The team score (molar mass) is not the score of one player times the number of players, but the sum of each player’s score multiplied by how many times they appear in the lineup.

## Worked example  
Calculate the molar mass of glucose \( \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \).

1. Atomic masses (approx.):  
   \(M_{\text{C}} = 12.01\), \(M_{\text{H}} = 1.008\), \(M_{\text{O}} = 16.00\).

2. Multiply by subscripts:  
   \(6 \times 12.01 = 72.06\)  
   \(12 \times 1.008 = 12.096\)  
   \(6 \times 16.00 = 96.00\)

3. Sum:  
   \[
   M = 72.06 + 12.096 + 96.00 = 180.156 \ \text{g/mol}.
   \]
