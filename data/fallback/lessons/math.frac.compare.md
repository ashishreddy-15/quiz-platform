## The idea
When we compare two fractions, we want to know which one is larger or smaller.  
If the fractions have the same denominator, the one with the bigger numerator is larger because it has more of the same‑size pieces.  
If the fractions have the same numerator, the one with the **smaller denominator** is larger because each piece is bigger.  
When denominators are different, we can change the fractions so they have a common denominator (or a common numerator) and then compare the numerators.

## Why your answer happened
You thought that a fraction with a bigger denominator is always bigger. That feeling is natural—seeing “3/8” versus “3/4”, the numbers 8 and 4 look bigger, so it seemed “bigger denominator = bigger fraction.” The mistake comes from looking at the denominator alone and forgetting that the denominator tells us how many pieces the whole is split into.

## Analogy
Think of a basketball game where the team’s score is the numerator and the number of quarters played is the denominator.

- **Score** = how many points the team has (numerator).  
- **Quarters played** = how many parts the game is divided into (denominator).

A team that scores 8 points in 4 quarters ( $8/4$ ) is doing better than a team that scores 8 points in 8 quarters ( $8/8$ ) because each quarter the first team averages more points. So, *the number of quarters (denominator) is like the size of each piece of the game*—more quarters mean each piece is smaller.

## Worked example
Compare $\displaystyle \frac{3}{5}$ and $\displaystyle \frac{2}{3}$.

1. Find a common denominator. The least common multiple of $5$ and $3$ is $15$.
2. Convert each fraction:
   \[
   \frac{3}{5} = \frac{3 \times 3}{5 \times 3} = \frac{9}{15},\qquad
   \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2 \times 5}{3 \times 5} = \frac{10}{15}
   \]
3. Now the denominators are the same, so compare the numerators: $9 < 10$.
4. Therefore $\displaystyle \frac{3}{5} < \frac{2}{3}$.

## Try this
Which fraction is larger: $\displaystyle \frac{4}{7}$ or $\displaystyle \frac{5}{9}$?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
Find a common denominator (LCM of 7 and 9 is 63):

\[
\frac{4}{7} = \frac{4 \times 9}{7 \times 9} = \frac{36}{63},\qquad
\frac{5}{9} = \frac{5 \times 7}{9 \times 7} = \frac{35}{63}
\]

Since $36 > 35$, $\displaystyle \frac{4}{7}$ is larger.
