## The idea
Gravity is a force that pulls every mass toward the center of the Earth.  
Weight is the measure of that force on an object.  
Mathematically, \(W = mg\), where \(m\) is mass and \(g \approx 9.8\;\text{m/s}^2\).  
Because \(g\) is the same everywhere near Earth’s surface, all objects accelerate at the same rate \(g\) when air resistance is negligible.

## Why your answer happened
You likely answered that heavier objects fall faster.  
That idea feels right because in everyday life we see a heavy ball drop a bit quicker than a feather in still air.  
The trick is that the feather’s slower fall is due to air resistance, not gravity itself.  Gravity acts equally on both.

## Analogy
Think of a soccer match:  
- The **gravity** is like the referee’s whistle that calls every player to run.  
- The **weight** is the player’s own mass determining how hard the whistle pushes them.  
Every player hears the whistle at the same moment, but their speed on the field depends on their own weight (mass).  The whistle (gravity) doesn’t favor one player over another.

## Worked example
A 10 kg box and a 2 kg box are dropped from the same height.  
Weight of the 10 kg box:  
\[
W_{10}=10\,\text{kg}\times 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2=98\,\text{N}
\]
Weight of the 2 kg box:  
\[
W_{2}=2\,\text{kg}\times 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2=19.6\,\text{N}
\]
Both boxes accelerate at  
\[
a = g = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2
\]
So they hit the ground simultaneously (ignoring air).

## Try this
A 5 kg ball and a 15 kg ball are dropped from the same height in a vacuum.  
Which ball reaches the ground first?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
They reach the ground at the same time because gravity accelerates both equally.  
</details>
