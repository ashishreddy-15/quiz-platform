## The idea  
Free fall means an object moves only under the influence of gravity, with no air resistance or other forces. In that ideal situation the acceleration is constant, $a = g \approx 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$ downward, no matter how fast the object is falling or how long it has been falling.

## Why your answer happened  
You likely answered that the acceleration changes as the object speeds up. That idea comes from everyday experience: a ball thrown up slows down, stops, then speeds up again. In free fall the only force is gravity, so the rate of change of velocity stays the same. The feeling that acceleration should vary is natural when you think of a ball being pushed or pulled, but in pure free fall it does not.

## Analogy  
Think of a soccer ball kicked straight up in a perfect vacuum. The ball’s “speed” is like the ball’s height above the ground. The “downward pull” of gravity is like the referee’s whistle that keeps the ball’s motion the same every second. The whistle’s constant tone (the same speed each beat) represents the constant $g$; it doesn’t get louder or softer as the ball goes higher or lower.

## Worked example  
A stone is dropped from a 45 m cliff.  
Initial velocity: $v_0 = 0$.  
Acceleration: $a = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$.  
Time to hit the ground:  

$$
y = v_0 t + \tfrac12 a t^2 \implies 45 = \tfrac12(9.8)t^2 \\
t^2 = \frac{90}{9.8}\approx 9.18 \implies t \approx 3.03\ \text{s}.
$$

Final velocity:  

$$
v = v_0 + a t = 0 + 9.8(3.03) \approx 29.7\ \text{m/s}.
$$

Notice $a$ stayed $9.8$ throughout.

## Try this  
A skydiver jumps from a 200 m platform.  
Assume no air resistance.  
What is the speed just before hitting the ground?  

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
Use $v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a y$:  
$v = \sqrt{0 + 2(9.8)(200)} \approx 62.6\ \text{m/s}$.
</details>
