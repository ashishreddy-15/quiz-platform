## The idea
Newton’s first law states that an object will maintain its current state of motion—either staying still or moving at a constant speed in a straight line—unless a net external force acts on it. “State of motion” means both speed *and* direction. If the forces cancel out (net force = 0), the object’s velocity does not change.

## Why your answer happened
You wrote that a moving object *needs* a force to keep moving. That feels reasonable because in everyday life we often see objects slow down unless we keep pushing them (like a bike coasting). The hidden factor is friction or air resistance, which are forces that oppose motion. When those forces are removed, the object would keep moving forever.

## Analogy
Think of a soccer ball gliding on a perfectly smooth, friction‑free field:

- **The ball’s motion** is like the ball’s velocity.
- **No net force** is like the field being perfectly smooth—nothing pushes or pulls the ball.
- **Friction or a player’s kick** is like an external force that changes the ball’s speed or direction.

On the ideal field, once the ball is kicked, it would roll forever at the same speed and straight line, because no net force acts on it.

## Worked example
A hockey puck slides on a frictionless ice surface.

1. Initial velocity: $v_0 = 5\ \text{m/s}$ to the east.  
2. Forces acting: only the normal force and gravity, which cancel vertically, so the horizontal net force $F_{\text{net}} = 0$.
3. Apply Newton’s first law:  

\[
F_{\text{net}} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{dp}{dt}=0 \;\Longrightarrow\; v(t)=v_0 = 5\ \text{m/s}
\]

4. After 10 s, the puck’s position is  

\[
x = v_0 t = 5\ \text{m/s} \times 10\ \text{s} = 50\ \text{m}
\]

The puck keeps moving east at 5 m/s the whole time because no net force acts horizontally.

## Try this
A basketball rolls across a perfectly smooth gym floor at 2 m/s north. No one touches it and there is no air resistance. What will its speed and direction be after 15 seconds?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
Speed remains **2 m/s** and direction stays **north**; the ball’s velocity is unchanged because the net force is zero.</details>
