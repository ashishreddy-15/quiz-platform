## The idea  
Proportional reasoning lets you find an unknown value when two quantities keep the same ratio.  
If \(a\) is to \(b\) as \(c\) is to \(x\), then  

\[
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{x}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{b\,c}{a}.
\]

The unknown is found by scaling with a constant multiplier (the unit rate).

## Why your answer happened  
You likely treated the problem as if you could add or subtract the numbers instead of multiplying.  
When the question asked for the number of points a player would score after a certain number of games, you might have written something like “points + games = answer.”  
That feels reasonable because adding looks easier, but it ignores the fact that the relationship is *multiplicative*—each game contributes the same number of points.

## Analogy  
Think of a soccer team scoring a constant number of goals per match.  
- **The constant goals per match** is like the *unit rate* (the multiplier).  
- **The total matches played** is like the *known quantity* (the \(c\) in the formula).  
- **The total goals scored** is like the *unknown* (\(x\)).  
So, “total goals = goals per match × number of matches.”

## Worked example  
A basketball player averages \(15\) points per game.  
How many points will he score after \(8\) games?

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{15\ \text{points}}{1\ \text{game}} &= \frac{x\ \text{points}}{8\ \text{games}}\\[4pt]
x &= \frac{15 \times 8}{1}\\[4pt]
x &= 120.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus, the player will score \(120\) points.

## Try this  
A soccer team scores an average of \(3\) goals in each match.  
If they play \(10\) matches, how many goals will they score?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>
\(3 \times 10 = 30\) goals.  
</details>
